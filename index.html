Pocket Guide: Simple Past for Tech Devs (A1 ➔ A2)

The 4 golden rules to never make a mistake in Daily Standups:

RULE 1

Regular Verbs (-ed)

  • Standard: add -ed: work ➔ worked, test ➔ tested.
  • Ending in -e: add only -d: close ➔ closed, save ➔ saved.
  • CVC (consonant + vowel + consonant): double final consonant: stop ➔ stopped.
RULE 2

Irregular Verbs in Tech

  • have ➔ had ("We had a sprint review")
  • write ➔ wrote ("Sarah wrote the backend test")
  • run ➔ ran ("The pipeline ran successfully")
  • find ➔ found ("We found a memory leak")
  • see ➔ saw ("I saw the pull request")
RULE 3

The Golden Rule of DID / DIDN'T

The auxiliaries did and didn't already carry the past tense. The main verb MUST RETURN TO THE BASE INFINITIVE!

❌ I didn't pushed the code.
✅ I didn't push the code.
❌ Did you reviewed the PR?
✅ Did you review the PR?
RULE 4

Past Tense of Verb To Be

  • WAS: I / He / She / It ("The server was down.")
  • WERE: You / We / They ("We were in a meeting.")
  • Caution: Never use "did be" or "didn't be"! ("The server wasn't responding").

SLIDE 01 // VISUAL FOUNDATION

The Cognitive Shift: Vector & Direction (TO) vs Benefit & Favor (FOR)

In Portuguese, "para" (or "pra") is used indiscriminately: "fui para casa", "café para você", "ferramenta para teste". In English, the preposition defines the exact nature of the relationship: either Direction / Destination (TO) or Benefit / Favor / Utility (FOR).

➔ TO • VECTOR, DIRECTION & DESTINATION

Indicates physical motion, data transmission, directed communication, or path endpoint.

go to the office send email to Carlos talk to the team connect to database
"The packet travels to the server."
🎁 FOR • BENEFIT, FAVOR & UTILITY

Indicates who receives the advantage, who is assisted or substituted, or intended utility.

made coffee for you review the PR for Sarah good for production fixed it for the client
"The dev wrote a script for the team."
💡 THE VECTOR TEST

How to decide in 2 seconds:

  • Is there movement or transfer toward a destination? If something travels or delivers to an endpoint, use TO.
  • Is someone assisted or receiving benefit? If someone receives a favor or you perform work on their behalf, use FOR.
  • Mental substitution test: Test with "toward / to the recipient" (TO) vs "on behalf of / for the benefit of" (FOR).
🧠 Mental anchor: "I sent the ticket to Alex" (Alex received it). "I closed the ticket for Alex" (I helped Alex by doing it for him).
SLIDE 02 // PURPOSE & INTENTION

The Golden Rule of Purpose: TO + Base Verb vs FOR + -ing / Noun

The #1 mistake made by non-native developers in international standups is saying "I joined the call for discuss..." ❌. Master this essential distinction:

🎯 HUMAN INTENTION (ACTION)

TO + Base Verb

When you explain why YOU performed an action using a verb:

✅ "I opened a PR to fix the login bug."
❌ for fix / for to fix (Critical error!)
✅ "We scheduled a sync to align sprint goals."
✅ "I logged in early to test the deploy pipeline."
✅ "Refactored the query to reduce latency."
Formula: Human intention ➔ TO + Base Verb
⚙️ OBJECT / TOOL FUNCTION

FOR + Verbo em -ing ou Substantivo

When explaining the purpose or function of a tool, service, or resource:

✅ "This microservice is used for parsing logs."
❌ to parsing (Nunca misture to + -ing aqui!)
✅ "Redis is an in-memory store for caching."
✅ "We allocated budget for new servers." (Substantivo)
✅ "I bought a headset for meetings." (Substantivo)
Formula: Tool utility ➔ FOR + -ing / Noun
⚡
O Duelo Perfeito: "I use Docker to containerize my apps." (My personal action) vs "Docker is a tool for containerizing apps." (Tool function)
SLIDE 03 // VERB PAIRINGS & THE EXPLAIN BUG

Transfer Verbs vs Service & The "Explain" Bug

Certain verbs in English are magnetically linked to TO or FOR. Master these essential software engineering patterns:

TO (Transfer / Destination)

Direct Communication & Motion

  • send to (send to recipient)
  • give to (hand over to)
  • forward to (forward to)
  • speak / talk to (talk to person)
  • listen to (ouvir a)
  • reply to (responder a)
"I forwarded the AWS credentials to Felipe."
FOR (Service & Favors)

Help, Delegation & Beneficiary

  • do a favor for (fazer favor)
  • review PR for (review on someone's behalf)
  • build / develop for (develop for client/team)
  • buy / order for (purchase on behalf)
  • save for (reserve for)
  • fix for (resolve for teammate)
"Can you merge this hotfix for me while I'm away?"
⚠️ ALERTA VERMELHO

O Maior Bug: Verbo EXPLAIN

In English, the verb explain NEVER accepts "explain me" or "explain for me"!

❌ "Explain for me this bug"
❌ "Explain me the code"
✅ "Explain this TO ME"
"Could you explain the Kafka architecture to the team?"
SLIDE 04 // DURATION & OPINION

Time Duration & The Nuance: "To me" vs "For me"

How to quantify outage duration in hours and minutes, and express opinions diplomatically in standups.

⏱️ MEDIDAS DE TEMPO

Continuous Duration vs Boundary Limits

FOR + DURATION / PERIOD

Expresses how long an action lasted or has been running continuously:

for 45 minutes for two hours for 3 days for five years
"The payment service was offline for 30 minutes."
FROM ... TO ... (LIMITES)

Expressa o marco inicial e o marco final de um intervalo:

from 9 AM to 5 PM from sprint 1 to sprint 4
"Our maintenance window is from midnight to 4 AM."
🧠 PONTO DE VISTA vs IMPACTO

"To me" vs "For me": The Opinion Test

TO ME
In my opinion / From my perspective (Subjective):
"To me, this design looks much cleaner."
"It seems to me that the query is unindexed."
FOR ME
In my case / Affects my routine or workload:
"Does 2:30 PM work for you?" (Na sua agenda?)
"Working remotely is very productive for me."
"This ticket is too difficult for me alone."

Diagnosis: Personal intention with a base verb requires to + base verb (to align). Saying "for align" is a classic false friend from Portuguese.

TICKET #502 • CODE REVIEW
❌ "Could the senior engineer please explain for me the OAuth callback flow?"
✅ "Could the senior engineer please explain to me the OAuth callback flow?"

Diagnosis: The verb explain strictly requires directional preposition to for the listener (explain to me).

TICKET #503 • OUTAGE POST-MORTEM
❌ "The payment gateway was completely unresponsive to 45 minutes."
✅ "The payment gateway was completely unresponsive for 45 minutes."

Diagnosis: Elapsed continuous time duration requires for (for 45 minutes). To indicates destinations or endpoints, not duration.

TICKET #504 • DEPLOYMENT HANDOFF
❌ "I forwarded the new production API credentials for the frontend team."
✅ "I forwarded the new production API credentials to the frontend team."

Diagnosis: Verbs of transmission and forwarding (forward, send, pass) take to for the recipient. Using for would imply sending on someone's behalf as a proxy.

SLIDE 01 // VISUAL FOUNDATION

The Cognitive Shift: The Inverted Prepositions Pyramid

In Portuguese, we use variations of "em" for countries, days, and hours indiscriminately. In English, prepositions define SCALE (Dimension & Geometric Precision).

IN • O MAIOR / GERAL
3D • Enclosed Space / Broad Container • Centuries, Years, Months • Countries & Cities
ON • INTERMEDIATE
2D • Surfaces & Lines • Days of Week & Calendar Dates • Streets & Screens
AT • ULTRA-SPECIFIC
1D • Precise Point Target • Exact Clock Times • Civic Address with Number
💡 THE GOLDEN RULE

Pense como um Funil de Zoom

  • IN: Zoomed out on the world map or yearly calendar.
  • ON: Mid-level zoom on calendar days or road corridors/surfaces.
  • AT: Maximum zoom on the minute clock hand or exact street number.
🧠 Mental model: Never translate "em". Ask yourself: "how broad is the target?"
SLIDE 02 // TIME PYRAMID

Prepositions of TIME: From Centuries to the Exact Minute

The temporal pyramid determines the preposition based on the scope of the time period.

IN (Geral / Longo)

Centuries, Years, Months & Periods

  • in 2025 / in 1999
  • in November / in July
  • in the summer / in winter
  • in the morning
  • in the afternoon
  • in the evening
"Our Q4 release happens in November."
ON (Mid-Scale / Days)

Days of the Week & Calendar Dates

  • on Monday / on Fridays
  • on October 21st
  • on my birthday
  • on Christmas Day
  • on weekdays
  • on the weekend (US)
"Sprint planning is always on Mondays."
AT (Pontual / Exato)

Clock Times & Fixed Moments

  • at 9:30 AM / at 4:00 PM
  • at noon / at midday
  • at midnight
  • at lunchtime
  • at the moment
  • at night ⚠️ (Classic exception!)
"Our standup begins sharp at 9:30 AM."
SLIDE 03 // PLACE PYRAMID

Prepositions of PLACE: From Countries to Building Numbers

Physical geometry defines whether a space is 3D (enclosed), 2D (surface), or 1D (GPS point).

IN (3D / Fechado)

Countries, Cities, Rooms & Enclosures

  • in Brazil / in Canada
  • in São Paulo / in London
  • in the meeting room
  • in the office building
  • in the kitchen
  • in the box / bag
"Our frontend lead is based in Berlin."
ON (2D / Surface)

Streets, Avenues, Surfaces & Screens

  • on Paulista Avenue
  • on 5th Street
  • on the table / desk (em cima)
  • on the 3rd floor (no andar)
  • on the wall
  • on the screen / on Slack
"The office is located on Market Street."
AT (1D / Ponto Exato)

Civic Address with Number & Meeting Points

  • at 450 Market St (with building number)
  • at the bus stop
  • at the airport
  • at the entrance
  • at home (em casa)
  • at work (no trabalho)
"The client meeting is at 100 Broadway."
SLIDE 04 // CHEAT CODES & TECH

Cheat Codes & Edge Cases: Transit and Cloud IT

Two high-yield mental models to master common questions in international standups.

🚌 MACETE DO TRANSPORTE

The "Aisle Walking" Rule

USE ON

If you can stand upright and walk down an aisle or sit astride:

on the bus on the train on the plane on the subway on a bike
USE IN

If you have to duck inside and remain seated:

in the car in a taxi in an Uber in a helicopter
💻 NO MUNDO DEV & TI

Cloud Infrastructure, Code & Workspaces

IN

"Tokens stored in the database" • "Variable defined in config.json"
ON
Platforms, Cloud, Hosts & Screens:
"PR opened on GitHub" • "Deployed on AWS" • "Shown on screen"

Focused workstation position:
"Working at home" • "Sitting at my desk"

SLIDE 01 // PHONETIC INTERFERENCE

The "Portuguese Stress Bug" in Tech Vocabulary

Why do Brazilian developers say "de-ve-lo-PER" or "tech-no-LO-gy"? In Portuguese, technical cognates almost always place primary stress on the suffix or final syllable. In English, word stress shifts backward!

🇧🇷 PORTUGUESE HABIT (STRESS AT END)

In Portuguese, stress naturally lands on the suffix or last syllable:

de-sen-vol-ve-DOR ➔ sounds like "de-ve-lo-PER" ❌ tec-no-lo-GI-a ➔ sounds like "tech-no-LO-gy" ❌ ar-qui-te-TU-ra ➔ sounds like "ar-chi-TEC-ture" ❌
🇺🇸 ENGLISH RULE (STRESS SHIFTS BACK)

In English, primary stress shifts earlier in the word:

de-VEL-op-er /dɪˈvel.ə.pər/ ✅ tech-NOL-o-gy /tekˈnɒl.ə.dʒi/ ✅ AR-chi-tec-ture /ˈɑːr.kə.tek.tʃər/ ✅
💡 Golden Rule: If you place word stress on the wrong syllable in English, native speakers often cannot recognize the word, even if every individual consonant and vowel is correct!
SLIDE 02 // MAGNETIC SUFFIXES

Magnetic Suffixes: The Syllable Before -LOGY and -ITY

Whenever a word ends in -logy or -ity, word stress is pulled strictly to the syllable immediately preceding the suffix!

SUFFIX -LOGY STRESS IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING
tech-NOL-o-gy (not tech-no-LO-gy!)
meth-od-OL-o-gy
bi-OL-o-gy
Why the mistake? In Portuguese we say "tec-no-lo-GI-a" with stress on GI. In English, stress is locked on NOL!
SUFFIX -ITY STRESS IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING
ca-PAC-i-ty (not ca-pa-CI-ty!)
pro-duc-TIV-i-ty (not pro-duc-ti-VI-ty!)
se-CUR-i-ty
re-li-a-BIL-i-ty
Key distinction: In Portuguese we say "ca-pa-ci-DA-de" and "pro-du-ti-vi-DA-de". In English, stress is pulled back to PAC and TIV!
SLIDE 03 // TECHNICAL VERBS

Verbs in -ATE and -IZE: Antepenultimate Stress

For verbs with 3 or more syllables ending in -ate or -ize, stress falls two syllables before the end (antepenultimate).

⚙️ AU-to-mate (not au-to-MATE)
Practical Example:

"We must AU-to-mate the test execution in CI/CD."

In PT: "au-to-ma-TI-zar". In English: AU-to-mate!
💬 com-MU-ni-cate (not com-mu-ni-CATE)
Practical Example:

"Devs must com-MU-ni-cate async on Slack."

In PT: "co-mu-ni-CAR". In English: stress is on MU!
🎯 pri-OR-i-tize (not pri-o-ri-TIZE)
Practical Example:

"The team decided to pri-OR-i-tize the critical bug."

In PT: "pri-o-ri-ZAR". In English: stress is on OR!
SLIDE 04 // 2-SYLLABLE TECH NOUNS

2-Syllable Tech Nouns: Stress on the First Syllable!

Over 85% of two-syllable nouns in English place primary stress on the first syllable. Here are the ones that cause the most confusion:

👥

COL-league /ˈkɒl.iːɡ/

In Portuguese we say "co-LE-ga" (stress on LE). In English, stress is firmly on the FIRST syllable: COL-league!

❌ Common trap: falar "col-LEAGUE" ou "co-LE-ga"
✅ Correct: my senior COL-league explained it to me
🚨

IN-ci-dent /ˈɪn.sɪ.dənt/

In Portuguese we say "in-ci-DEN-te". In English, stress is on IN, and the remaining syllables reduce smoothly.

❌ Common trap: saying "in-ci-DENT" with stress at the end
✅ Correct: we opened a high-priority IN-ci-dent
SLIDE 05 // THE SCHWA SECRET

The Reduced Neutral Vowel "Schwa" (/ə/): The Rhythm of English

Portuguese is a syllable-timed language where most vowels are clearly pronounced. English is a stress-timed language: any syllable that is NOT stressed reduces to the weak neutral vowel (/ə/ - schwa)!

Practical Example: de-VEL-op-er (/dɪˈvel.ə.pər/)

1. The first syllable de- is unstressed and reduces to /dɪ/
2. The second syllable VEL is stressed: louder, longer, higher pitch!
3. The third syllable -op- reduces to schwa /ə/
4. The fourth syllable -er reduces to r-colored schwa /pər/

If you say "de-ve-lo-PER", vowel reduction fails to occur, English sentence rhythm breaks down, and native listeners struggle to parse the word!
